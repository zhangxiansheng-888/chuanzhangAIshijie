# Performance recipes

Use these as starting chains. Adapt intensity, timing, asymmetry, shot size, and cultural context.

## Three-axis control

Describe performance using three independent controls:

1. Internal emotional pressure: how strongly the character feels the emotion
2. Outward expression amplitude: how visibly the face and body reveal it
3. Self-control: how strongly the character is suppressing or shaping the response

Example: emotional numbness can carry internal pressure 5/5, outward amplitude 1/5, and self-control 5/5. A breakdown can begin at pressure 5/5, amplitude 1/5, control 5/5, then end at pressure 5/5, amplitude 5/5, control 1/5.

Keep these numbers in control notes. Translate them into visible actions inside the production prompt.

## Same dialogue, six emotional readings

These recipes are derived from a 38.9 second portrait reference video in which the same adult character says the same one word line with six different intentions. The useful lesson is the ordering of visible channels.

### Playfully scolding

`side glance → suppressed smile at one lip corner → slight head tilt → small hand shield → softly deliver the line → smile residue`

Face details: eyes brighten before the mouth moves, cheeks lift mildly, lips stay closed at first, smile grows asymmetrically, blink or brief eye closure appears near the apex.

### Aggrieved and hurt

`eyes become moist → lips press together → breath catches → shoulders contract slightly → hand withdraws toward the chest → deliver the line through a restrained choke`

Face details: inner brows lift and draw together, lower lids tense, lips press then part, chin tightens, gaze alternates between the other person and downward avoidance.

### Disappointed

`eyes brighten in expectation then dim → exhale sinks → shoulders lower → body retreats slightly → deliver the line quietly → gaze remains lowered`

Face details: focus drops before head movement, mouth loses tension rather than forming a dramatic frown, jaw releases, eyelids become heavier, recovery remains incomplete.

### Angry

`gaze hardens → jaw sets → breath grows heavier → body leans forward → fingers tighten → deliver the line sharply → hold the stare`

Face details: brows lower and knit, lower lids firm, nostrils may flare mildly, lips compress before the word, mouth opening is brief and forceful. Keep the eyes focused rather than broadly widened.

### Breaking down

`gaze fragments → breathing becomes irregular → body recoils → hands move into self-protection → voice tightens with crying pressure → the line breaks apart → face fails to recover`

Face details: inner brows rise sharply, lower lids tense, chin dimples, jaw drops during the cry, mouth corners pull unevenly, blinking becomes irregular. Reserve intensity 5 for the apex.

### Emotionally numb

`gaze empties → breath becomes shallow → shoulders sink → attention withdraws from the other person → voice turns very light with a trace of tremor → deliver the line flatly → remain detached`

Face details: reduce motion, soften focus, slow the blink, release the jaw slightly, keep lip movement minimal, delay the reaction after speaking.

## Additional reusable recipes

### Polite smile hiding suspicion

`social lip smile → eyes stay cool and fixed → one brow tightens slightly → smile fades before gaze moves away`

### Relief after danger

`stare holds for a beat → long exhale → eyelids soften → jaw releases → small uneven smile appears → eyes briefly close`

### Recognition turning to dread

`focus snaps onto the target → upper lids rise → mouth parts → inner brows lift and draw together → breath stops → gaze remains locked while the head retreats`

### Jealousy concealed as calm

`gaze flicks toward the rival → lips compress briefly → jaw tightens on one side → neutral expression returns too quickly → gaze avoids contact`

## Timing patterns

Choose a phase count that fits duration. Do not force every performance into the same number of stages.

### Subtle close-up, 3 seconds

1. 0.0 to 0.7 seconds: neutral baseline and gaze cue
2. 0.7 to 1.6 seconds: eyelid and brow onset
3. 1.6 to 2.3 seconds: mouth or jaw apex
4. 2.3 to 3.0 seconds: partial recovery with residue

### Dialogue close-up, 5 seconds

1. 0.0 to 1.0 seconds: listen and process
2. 1.0 to 2.0 seconds: emotional onset led by the eyes
3. 2.0 to 3.5 seconds: breath change and spoken line
4. 3.5 to 5.0 seconds: reaction residue and gaze shift

### Example six-beat emotional scene, 8 seconds

1. 0.0 to 1.2 seconds: environment and baseline
2. 1.2 to 2.5 seconds: trigger recognition
3. 2.5 to 4.0 seconds: resistance and physiological leakage
4. 4.0 to 5.6 seconds: facial and bodily escalation
5. 5.6 to 6.8 seconds: apex, line, or decision
6. 6.8 to 8.0 seconds: residue and motivated ending

Use this allocation only when all six beats are required. Shorten or extend the total duration after evaluating the actual trigger, interaction, dialogue, and ending.

### Still image

Freeze the face just after onset or at a restrained apex. Include evidence of the preceding moment, such as lips only beginning to part, moisture just collecting at the lower lid, or one brow reacting before the other.

## Failure corrections

| Failure | Correction |
|---|---|
| Generic emotion label | Add gaze, eyelid, brow, lip, jaw, and timing details |
| Overacting | Lower one channel by one intensity level and delay the body response |
| Frozen mask | Add blink preparation, breath, tiny jaw release, and partial recovery |
| Rubber mouth | Restrict mouth opening, define lip compression first, and shorten the apex |
| Doll eyes | Define a gaze target, lower lid tension, blink timing, and catchlight behavior |
| Symmetric synthetic smile | Lead with one lip corner and delay the opposite cheek |
| Identity drift | Lock facial proportions, age, skin texture, hairline, and makeup throughout |
| Emotion changes too abruptly | Add a neutral baseline, onset cue, and residue phase |
